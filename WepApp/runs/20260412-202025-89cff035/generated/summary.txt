--- Classes ---
  • Traumatic Brain Injury — A type of brain injury resulting from external mechanical forces, often leading to various neurological impairments.
  • Head Injury — An injury to the head that may involve the skull, brain, or both, often leading to significant morbidity.
  • Monitoring Data — Data collected from patients to monitor physiological parameters and outcomes in clinical settings.
  • Cerebral Perfusion Pressure — The pressure gradient driving blood flow to the brain, calculated as Blood Pressure minus Intracranial Pressure.
  • Secondary Insult — An additional injury or complication that occurs after the initial injury, potentially worsening outcomes.
  • Neurological Injury — An injury that affects the nervous system, including the brain and spinal cord.
  • Substance Abuse — The harmful or hazardous use of psychoactive substances, including alcohol and illicit drugs.
  • Coma — A state of prolonged unconsciousness where a person cannot be awakened and fails to respond to stimuli.
  • Intracranial Pressure — The pressure inside the skull, which can affect brain function and is monitored in patients with brain injuries.
  • GCS Assessment — A clinical scale used to assess a patient's level of consciousness after a brain injury.
  • Mean Arterial Pressure — The pressure of circulating blood on the walls of blood vessels, an important vital sign.
  • Central Venous Pressure — The pressure in the thoracic vena cava near the right atrium, used to assess right heart function.
  • Core Monitoring Parameter — A mandatory physiological variable collected at minimum once per minute.
  • Optional Monitoring Parameter — An additional physiological variable collected when clinically available.
  • Heart Rate — The number of cardiac contractions per minute, a core physiological monitoring parameter in neuro-ICU.
  • Respiration Rate — The number of breaths per minute, measured via impedance plethysmography or ventilator.
  • Arterial Oxygen Saturation — Arterial oxygen saturation measured by pulse oximetry, indicating blood oxygenation levels.
  • Temperature — Core body temperature measured at rectal, oesophageal, bladder, or skin sites.
  • End-Tidal Carbon Dioxide — End-tidal carbon dioxide concentration, reflecting ventilation adequacy.
  • Jugular Venous Oxygen Saturation — Jugular venous oxygen saturation, an indicator of cerebral oxygen consumption.
  • Transcranial Doppler — Transcranial Doppler ultrasonography, used to assess cerebral blood flow velocity.
  • Brain Tissue Oxygen — Partial pressure of brain tissue oxygen, measuring local cerebral oxygenation.
  • Brain Temperature — Temperature measured directly within brain tissue, distinct from core body temperature.
  • Microdialysis — A sampling technique for measuring extracellular brain chemistry metabolites.
  • Vasopressors — Medications used to constrict blood vessels and increase blood pressure.
  • Hypotension — A condition in which blood pressure is abnormally low, which can lead to inadequate blood flow to organs.
  • Therapy — Medical treatment provided to patients to manage their condition.
  • Demographic Data — Data related to the characteristics of the patient population.
  • Intracranial Hypertension — An increase in pressure within the cranial cavity, which can lead to severe complications in brain injury patients.
  • Clinical Research Form (CRF) — A document used to collect data from participants in clinical trials.
  • Outcome Data — Information collected regarding the results of treatment or management interventions in patients.
  • Management — The process of planning and executing treatments and interventions for patients.
  • Treatment Data — Information regarding the interventions applied to patients during their care.
  • Sepsis — A life-threatening condition caused by the body's response to an infection, leading to tissue damage and organ failure.
