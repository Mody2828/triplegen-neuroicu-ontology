--- Classes ---
  • Patient — Individual receiving neurointensive care for traumatic brain injury (TBI).
  • Demographic Data — Demographic and clinical admission data collected at enrolment: age, sex, injury classification, medical history, GCS scores, pupil assessment, and CT-Scan assessment. [P1]
  • Session — A continuous monitoring session for a patient in the ICU, comprising an ordered sequence of timepoints. [P2]
  • Timepoint — A discrete moment in the minute-by-minute monitoring time-series, associated with a set of sensor readings. BrainIT collects at 1-sample/minute minimum. [P1, P2]
  • Monitoring Data — Abstract superclass for all physiological monitoring parameters recorded in the BrainIT dataset.
  • Core Monitoring Parameter — Minimum required minute-by-minute monitoring parameters for BrainIT Database inclusion. All participating centres must provide these. [P1]
  • Optional Monitoring Parameter — Extended monitoring parameters included in the BrainIT Database whenever monitored as part of a centre's routine clinical management. [P1]
  • Derived Parameter — Monitoring parameter computed from other measured parameters (e.g. CPP = MAP − ICP). [P1]
  • Heart Rate — Core BrainIT parameter. Source: ECG or Pulse Oximetry. [P1]
  • Respiration Rate — Core BrainIT parameter. Source: impedance plethysmography or ventilator. [P1]
  • Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) — Core BrainIT parameter. Key determinant of Cerebral Perfusion Pressure. Inter-centre zero-reference standardisation is critical. [P1]
  • Intracranial Pressure (ICP) — Core BrainIT parameter. Monitoring site: intraventricular, intraparenchymal, or subdural. Technology: catheter-tip or fluid-filled catheter-transducer. [P1]
  • SaO2 — Core BrainIT parameter. Arterial oxygen saturation by Pulse Oximetry. [P1]
  • Temperature — Core BrainIT parameter. Source: rectal, oesophageal, bladder, or skin. [P1]
  • CVP — Central Venous Pressure. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • EtCO2 — End-tidal CO2. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • SjO2 — Jugular venous oxygen saturation. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • TCD — Transcranial Doppler. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • PbrO2 — Brain tissue oxygen partial pressure. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • Brain Temperature — Cerebral temperature. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • Microdialysis — Cerebral microdialysis. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • Peripheral Temperature — Peripheral (skin/tympanic) temperature. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • Cardiac Output — Cardiac output monitoring. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • NIBP — Non-invasive blood pressure. Optional BrainIT parameter. [P1]
  • Cerebral Perfusion Pressure (CPP) — Derived: CPP = MAP − ICP. Critical threshold ~70 mmHg. CPP-targeted therapy is a key BrainIT secondary-insult management concept. [P1]
  • Pressure Reactivity Index (PRx) — Derived index reflecting cerebrovascular pressure reactivity: moving correlation coefficient between slow waves of ABP and ICP. [P1]
  • Therapy — Any patient therapy or clinical intervention administered during the ICU stay. Abstract superclass.
  • Baseline Therapy — Routine ICU management delivered to all TBI patients. Collected as Intensive Care Management data with start/end times. [P1]
  • Secondary Insult Therapy — Non-surgical medical management specifically given to treat secondary insults. Each therapy must be assigned a therapy target from a standardised drop-down list. [P1]
  • Ventilation — Mechanical ventilation settings and management. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Sedation — Sedation level management. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Fluids — Fluid input and output management. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Nutrition — Nutritional support. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Vasopressors — Vasopressor agents used to raise arterial pressure. Applied both as baseline therapy and as secondary insult therapy targeting systemic hypotension or reduced CPP. [P1]
  • Antibiotics — Antibiotic therapy. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Analgesia — Analgesic therapy. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Paralysis — Neuromuscular paralysis agents. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Anti-hypertensives — Anti-hypertensive therapy. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Anti-pyretics — Anti-pyretic therapy. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Hypothermia Therapy — Therapeutic hypothermia / induced cooling. Baseline therapy. [P1]
  • Noradrenaline — Noradrenaline (norepinephrine). Specific vasopressor whose administration contextualises out-of-range heart rate readings during data quality assessment. [P2]
  • Adrenaline — Adrenaline (epinephrine). Specific vasopressor whose administration contextualises out-of-range heart rate and blood pressure readings. [P2]
  • Secondary Insult Treatment — Coded therapy category with an assigned therapy target (e.g. arterial pressors targeting systemic hypotension or reduced CPP). [P1]
  • Arterial Pressors — Arterial pressure agents used in secondary insult therapy. Therapy target: systemic hypotension OR reduced CPP secondary to raised ICP. [P1]
  • Osmotics — Osmotic agents (mannitol, hypertonic saline). Secondary insult therapy. [P1]
  • Barbiturates — Barbiturate therapy (thiopental). Secondary insult therapy. [P1]
  • Steroids — Corticosteroid therapy. Secondary insult therapy. [P1]
  • Surgical Procedure — Neurosurgical procedure performed on the patient during ICU stay. [P1]
  • ICP Sensor Placement — Placement of ICP monitoring sensor (intraventricular, intraparenchymal, or subdural). [P1]
  • Evacuation of Mass Lesion — Surgical evacuation of intracranial mass lesion (craniotomy). [P1]
  • Skull Fracture Elevation — Surgical elevation of depressed skull fracture. [P1]
  • Extra Ventricular Drain Placement — Placement of external ventricular drain (EVD) for CSF drainage. [P1]
  • Decompressive Craniectomy — Decompressive craniectomy to relieve intracranial pressure. [P1]
  • Removal of Foreign Body — Surgical removal of intracranial foreign body. [P1]
  • Anterior Fossa Repair — Anterior fossa repair for CSF leak. [P1]
  • Condition — Abstract superclass for all clinical conditions: secondary insults, medical disorders, and physiological states monitored or treated during ICU care.
  • Secondary Insult — Adverse physiological event occurring in TBI patients during ICU management despite baseline care. Over 90% of TBI patients exhibit one or more insults during their stay. [P1]
  • Intracranial Hypertension — Raised ICP constituting a secondary insult. Drives CPP-targeted therapy. [P1]
  • Systemic Hypotension — Reduced systemic blood pressure. Secondary insult in TBI; targeted by arterial pressor therapy. [P1]
  • Arterial Hypotension — Low arterial blood pressure; closely related to systemic hypotension in the TBI secondary insult framework. [P1]
  • Jugular Venous Desaturation — SjO2 < 50%. A secondary insult associated with aggressive CPP management (>70 mmHg). [P1]
  • Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome — ARDS. Secondary complication linked to aggressive CPP-targeted therapy in TBI. [P1]
  • Hypertension — Elevated blood pressure. Medical disorder that can contextualise out-of-range readings and reclassify probable errors to possible errors in ICU data quality assessment. 91% reclassification rate in general ICU. [P2]
  • Hypotension — Low blood pressure. Medical disorder relevant to ICU data quality assessment. 80.5–85.6% of out-of-range values in TBI and general ICU datasets may be affected. [P2]
  • Sepsis — Systemic infection response. Provides clinical context: an HR of 120 that appears erroneous may be a genuine symptom of sepsis, reclassifying the error. [P2]
  • Clinical Assessment — Structured clinical evaluation performed at admission and during the ICU stay. Abstract superclass. [P1]
  • GCS Assessment — Glasgow Coma Scale. Collected at pre-neurosurgical hospital admission and at neurosurgical admission. Also collected as time-series in the ICU management dataset. [P1]
  • Pupil Assessment — Pupil size (small / normal / large) and responsiveness (reacting / unreacting) recorded individually for each eye. [P1]
  • CT Scan Assessment — CT scan classification using TCDB nomenclature. Both first and worst CT collected. Covers main lesion type, volume (>/<25 ml), and cistern state. [P1]
  • Outcome — Patient outcome measure. Abstract superclass. [P1]
  • GOSe Outcome — Extended Glasgow Outcome Scale (8-point) at 6 months post-discharge from the neurosurgical centre. Collected by structured interview with patient, relative, or GP. [P1]
  • Laboratory Values — Routine laboratory tests performed during ICU management. Includes blood gases, biochemistry, and haematology. Recorded daily alongside minute-by-minute monitoring. [P1]
  • Blood Gases — Arterial blood gas analysis. Recorded routinely in ICU management data. [P1]
  • Biochemistry — Daily biochemistry panel: Sodium (Na), Potassium (K+), Glucose. [P1]
  • Haematology — Daily haematology panel: Haemoglobin, White Blood Cell Count, Haematocrit. [P1]
  • Sodium — Serum sodium (Na). Daily biochemistry value. [P1]
  • Potassium — Serum potassium (K+). Daily biochemistry value. [P1]
  • Glucose — Blood glucose. Daily biochemistry value. [P1]
  • Haemoglobin — Haemoglobin concentration. Daily haematology value. [P1]
  • White Blood Cell Count — White cell count (WBC). Daily haematology value. [P1]
  • Haematocrit — Packed cell volume. Daily haematology value. [P1]
  • Nursing Intervention — Routine nursing care, physiotherapy, or bedside clinical activities annotated in the ICU management time-series. [P1]
  • Routine Nursing Care — Eye/mouth care, ET tube suction, bed care. Coded and annotated in ICU management data. [P1]
  • Physiotherapy — Physiotherapy sessions annotated in ICU management data. [P1]
  • Bedside Intervention — Line insertion/renewal, transducer calibration, x-ray. Annotated in ICU management data. [P1]
  • Patient Transport — Transport to and from Theatre or CT-Scan. Annotated in ICU management data. [P1]
  • Intensive Care Management — ICU management data category. Baseline medical management and monitoring of clinical status occurring more than once during the patient's ICU stay. Collected by menu-driven PC software with start/end times. [P1]
  • Observation — A single measurement or reading recorded at a Timepoint for a given Parameter. Linked to the Sensor that produced it and assessable for data quality. [P2]
  • Parameter — A physiological or clinical variable that is measured and recorded (e.g. Heart Rate, ICP, MAP). Corresponds to pd:Parameter in the Patient Data ontology. [P2]
  • Sensor — A machine or sensor used to record physiological parameters. Described by its sensing process, the property it observes, and its measurement capability (accuracy, sampling rate). [P2]
  • Data Quality Assessment — Automated evaluation of ICU data quality using semantic web technologies, considering acceptable value ranges, sensor accuracy, data completeness, and clinical context (medical disorders and treatments). [P2]
  • Possible Error — PoE: a reading outside acceptable range that is contextually explained by a concurrent medical disorder or recent treatment (reclassified from PrE). [P2]
  • Probable Error — PrE: a reading outside its acceptable range with no contextual clinical explanation from disorders or treatments. More likely a genuine data error. [P2]
